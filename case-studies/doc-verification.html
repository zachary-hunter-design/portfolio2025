From Four Identical Cards to One

International check-in at Breeze, from a messy travel API to one page per booking

Executive Summary

International check-in pulls document rules from Timatic, an industry travel API. The old flow asked each traveler separately, so a family of four flying to the Dominican Republic acknowledged the same entry form four times.

I redesigned it as one page for the whole booking: shared requirements show once, and anything that applies to one person says so. Then I took the same data public, as a lookup anyone can use before they book, and validated it with 21 people.

Role:

UX/UI Designer

Duration:

March to July 2026

Responsibilities:

Research, system design, prototyping, validation testing

The Problem

What Comes Back From the API

Timatic answers per traveler, across a dozen rule codes. Some results need the Guest to do something, most don't, and nothing tells you whether a form is one per family or one per person.

The old flow showed every result inside a step for each traveler. Our engineering manager put it plainly:

The hard part is mixed bookings. A couple on U.S. and Ukrainian passports shares one entry form, but only Olena needs an entry authorization. The page has to say "everyone" and "just Olena" without making "everyone" feel skippable.

The Design

One Page Per Booking

Every traveler's results go into one list. Identical requirements collapse into a single card, tagged with who it applies to, and check-in waits until each one is acknowledged.

Four Calls That Shaped It

Call What I Did Why
Booking, not traveler Moved documents out of the per-traveler steps into one page A family of four got four identical cards. The engineering manager confirmed it was real friction
Collapse matches Merged results with the same rule and the same text into one card Timatic can't say if a form is per family. Identical results are the signal, so the missing flag stops mattering
Only what's actionable Showed entry forms, entry authorizations with a link, and proof of onward travel Everything else is already satisfied, handled by an agent at the airport, or out of scope
Scope by shape "All travelers" as quiet text, named travelers as pills The original two-pill design scored 70% in a 5-second test (n=20), under my 80% bar

AI helped me run the dedup rule across 29 real Timatic scenarios and generate a dozen tag variants in one sitting. Which variant, when to stop, and what to leave off the page were my calls.

Research

Three Kinds of Evidence

29

real Timatic scenarios across routes and passports, which set the dedup and show-or-skip rules

11

people in the March MVP test, surfacing 7 critical dev bugs and 8 UX issues

70%

The Build

Reviewing It as a Guest

I reviewed the build (MR 5613) against the spec. Most of it matched, and I accepted a few small changes, like the page title becoming "Additional Travel Requirements."

Walking it as a Guest is what caught the real bug: "Complete Later" dropped you on Manage Trip with no way back. I raised it as an expectation ("I'd expect to be able to return to it") rather than a code review note, and let engineering own the fix.

The Miss

The build shipped the original tag design, the one that scored 70%. That one's on me: I didn't update Figma before engineering started. The fix is queued, and the new tag still needs its own test before I'd call it proven.

What Came Next

A Public Lookup, Before You Book

Check-in only helps once you've booked. So I designed a public page: enter a route, a passport, and a country of residence, and see what Timatic says you'll need.

One call carried over from check-in: the page tells you what's required, but never says "you're all set," because it can't know your full situation. Designing it also meant correcting the requirements doc. Residence had to become a required input, because where you live changes visa rules.

Tested With 21 People

I set the pass lines before testing. All three research questions cleared them.

100%

got to a result without help

~90%

understood a result with several documents

95%

edited their search on mobile without confusion

The one real gap: five people asked how to actually get the visa. Breeze can't issue one, so the page states the requirement and points to government sources instead of pretending to solve it.

I handed it off as 20 Figma frames, desktop and mobile. Engineering picked up the build in late July.

What I'd Keep

  • Update the source of truth before handoff. A test result that never reaches Figma doesn't reach the build.
  • Raise gaps as experience questions. "I'd expect to get back here" lands better than pointing at a line of code, and keeps me in my lane.
