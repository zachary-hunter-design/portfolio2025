Four Travelers, One Entry Form

International check-in at Breeze, from a per-traveler API to one page per booking

Executive Summary

International check-in at Breeze gets its document rules from Timatic, the travel industry's requirements API. It answers one traveler at a time, so a family of four flying to the Dominican Republic confirmed the same entry form four times.

I redesigned it as one page for the whole booking: shared requirements show once, and anything that applies to one person says who. It's been live in check-in since May 2026. It also replaced document rules that were hardcoded airport by airport, so new routes work without rework.

Then I took the same data public, as a lookup anyone can use before they book. I tested it with 21 people, and it's in build now.

Role:

UX/UI Designer

Duration:

March to July 2026

Responsibilities:

Research, system design, prototyping, validation testing

The Problem

Four Times, the Same Form

The old flow put documents inside each traveler's check-in steps. Every traveler got their own results, even when they were identical. A family of four going to the Dominican Republic saw the same entry form four times and had to confirm it four times.

Our engineering manager flagged it first: one person fills out that form for the family, so asking four times was pure friction.

The harder case is a mixed booking. A couple on U.S. and Ukrainian passports shares one entry form, but only Olena needs an entry authorization. The page has to say "everyone" and "just Olena" without making "everyone" feel skippable.

What the API Actually Says

A Dozen Codes, Three Things to Do

Before this, document rules were hardcoded airport by airport, each time Breeze added an international route. Timatic already knows the rules, but it answers per traveler, in a dozen rule codes, and most of them ask nothing of the guest. It also never says whether a form is one per family or one per person.

I ran 29 real scenarios across routes and passports and sorted every answer by one question: does the guest have to do something?

Timatic Says The Guest Sees What They Do
An entry form is required An Entry Form card Fill it out, then confirm
No visa needed with an online authorization An Entry Authorization card with the link Get the authorization, then confirm
A return ticket is required A Proof of Onward Travel card Confirm they have one
No visa needed, or a simple exemption Nothing Nothing to do
A visa is required and missing No card. Check-in sends them to an agent Sort it out at the airport
Notes and behind-the-scenes checks Nothing Nothing to do

Because the rules key off what Timatic returns, not which airport you fly to, a new route works without new rules.

The Design

One Page Per Booking

Documents moved out of each traveler's steps and onto one page for the whole booking. The rule that makes it work is simple: if everyone got the same answer, show it once. Timatic doesn't need to say whether a form is per family. Identical answers are the signal.

Anything that applies to one person carries their name, and check-in waits until each card is confirmed.

The Tag I'd Do Differently

The hardest call was telling "everyone" from "just Olena." The first design used two similar pills. In a 5-second test with 20 people, 70% read them right, under my 80% bar, so "everyone" really was easy to skim past. I designed a fix: a quiet "All travelers" label, and a pill only for a named traveler. That's the version in the image above.

But I didn't update Figma before engineering started, so the original tags are what shipped. Engineering built exactly what the spec said. A test result that never reaches Figma doesn't reach the build. Now I update the source of truth before handoff.

Live in Check-In

Walking It as a Guest

The booking page went live in May 2026. Before it did, I walked the build as a guest. That caught a dead end: "Complete Later" sent you to Manage Trip with no way back to the page. I raised it as an expectation, "I'd expect to be able to come back to this," and engineering fixed it.

Before You Book

A Public Lookup

Check-in only helps once you've booked. So I designed a public page: enter a route, your passport, and where you live, and see what you'll need.

Two calls shaped it. Where you live became a required input, which meant correcting the requirements doc, because residence changes visa rules. And the page tells you what's required but never says "you're all set," because it can't know your full situation.

Tested With 21 People

I set the pass lines before testing. All three research questions cleared them.

100%

got to a result without help

~90%

understood a result with several documents

95%

edited their search on mobile without confusion

The one real gap: five people asked how to actually get the visa. Breeze can't issue one, so the page states the requirement and points to government sources.

Where It Stands

  • One page per booking: live in check-in since May 2026
  • New international routes: no document rules to hardcode
  • The public lookup: designed, tested, and in build with engineering

One habit I'm keeping: raise gaps as experience questions. "I'd expect to get back here" lands better than pointing at a line of code, and it leaves the fix with the people who know the code best.
