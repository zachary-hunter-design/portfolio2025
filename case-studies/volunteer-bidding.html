Turning a 5 Percent Opt In Into an Offer People Trust

Breeze's volunteer offer for oversold flights, from research to production

Executive Summary

When a flight is oversold, Breeze asks Guests during check in if they'll take a different flight for compensation. About 5 percent even opened the offer. I ran the research, redesigned the flow, and validated it before it shipped.

In testing, comprehension went from 50 percent to 100 percent. Once it was live, completion rose 21 to 31 points at every airport for Guests who started the flow.

I stayed with it through V3: an option to cancel instead of fly later, set bid offers, and four more rounds of testing before handoff.

Role:

UX/UI Designer, UX Researcher

Duration:

One month, plus follow-ups through 2026

Responsibilities:

UX research, UX/UI design, prototyping

The Problem

Most Guests Said No Before They Knew What Was Offered

This started as a copy update: compensation was switching from BreezePoints to flight credits. Then I looked at the funnel.

5%

of invited Guests opened the offer

24%

of those picked a flight

<1%

of invited Guests placed a bid

I expected people to drop at flight selection, once they saw the alternates didn't fit their plans. They never got that far. Most said no at the invite, which pointed at how the offer was introduced, not the trade itself.

Check in is a get-it-done moment. The invite asked people to read a paragraph about a system they'd never seen.

Research

Watching Six People Try It

I ran a moderated think-aloud test of the live flow on our staging site.

Research Overview

Participants

  • Six people, ages 23 to 66
  • Mostly infrequent flyers
  • No prior Breeze experience

The Flow

  • Invite
  • Bid amount
  • Alternate flight
  • Confirm and success

Questions

  • Do people understand the offer?
  • Where do they get confused?
  • What stops them?

The Invite Read as a Hustle

Two of six thought it was a loyalty program. Others read it as something worse.

Bidding Felt Like Gambling

Only two of six could explain how bidding worked.

Flight Selection Raised More Questions

  • The original flight wasn't shown next to the alternates
  • Some alternates were long delays
  • It wasn't clear if a seat on the alternate was guaranteed
  • No sense of how many volunteers were needed

By Confirmation, People Backed Out

Several said they'd quit here and just check in. Timing, airport logistics, and guarantees were all still unclear, so it didn't feel worth the risk.

Design

Say Less on the Invite

Same facts, less than half the words. "Earn BreezePoints" became "Get Paid," and the fine print became two bullets.

Flights First, Then the Bid

People wanted to know if an alternate even worked before naming a price. The old flow asked for a bid first, so they were committing to a number for a flight they hadn't seen.

Reassure the Whole Way

Every step now carries the same line: your original flight stays booked until you're selected at the gate. Smaller fixes rode along: clearer selected states on flight cards, a simpler bid summary, and a cleaner credit picker.

In Production

The new flow went live in January. Here's August to November against January to March.

Airport Finished, Once Started Invite to Bid
TPA 19% → 48% 0.90% → 1.06%
MCO 16% → 47% 0.83% → 1.42%
SNA 35% → 64% 2.27% → 3.60%
PVU 33% → 54% 1.78% → 1.94%

SNA got 16 percent fewer invites and 33 percent more confirmed bids. Less traffic, more bids. That's the design doing the work, not volume.

Invite to bid stayed small everywhere. Once someone found a flight that worked, they finished. Whether a flight worked was never something design could fix.

What Came Next

More Ways to Say Yes

The redesign got people through the flow. It didn't fix the bigger problem: most Guests have somewhere to be. Over the next year the flow came back to me three more times.

Ask What I Did Status
Legal Disclosure Added denied-boarding terms at the bid step in as few words as the requirement allowed Shipped. Tested with 21 people, comprehension held
Offer to Cancel Gave cancel its own choice next to "fly later," not a spot on flight selection. One page, one job Designed, tested, handed off. Waiting on engineering
Bid Amounts Replaced the custom amount with four set offers, labeled 2x to 5x your fare Designed, tested, handed off

Testing Caught Something I Broke

I ran four unmoderated rounds over two days, about 160 people. An earlier V3 dropped the check that matters most: knowing you still show up for your original flight. One more round brought it back.

Wrong-gate answers went from 8 of 41 to 2 of 39. My first write-up said the final V3 beat production. It didn't, it matched it, so I corrected the headline.

The Real Limit

A full flight only needs a few volunteers, and agents take the lowest offers first, so most bids are never meant to be accepted. What matters is whether each full flight gets enough offers to pick from. That depends on the route: after picking a flight, 30% of Guests offered one alternate placed a bid, against 50% with nine or more. No screen makes a fixed plan flexible.

V3 is handed off. The build doesn't have a date yet.
